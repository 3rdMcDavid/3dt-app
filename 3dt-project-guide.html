Phase 4

Build & Review Cycle

Once the client's deposit clears and they submit their intake form, you build. This phase runs through up to three review rounds — Draft 1, Draft 2, and a Final — before the client gives written approval through the portal.

What the Admin App Does for You
Your Action What Happens Automatically
Intake submitted by client Dashboard shows "⚡ Intake received — build Draft 1" under Needs Attention
Click "Send Draft 1" in Project Hub Revision stage advances → email sent to client with portal link → client sees "Review Draft 1 →" CTA on their portal
Client submits feedback in portal Dashboard shows "⚡ Draft 1 feedback received — build Draft 2"
Click "Send Draft 2" in Project Hub Same as above but for round 2
Click "Send Final" in Project Hub Email sent — client sees "Approve Final →" on portal
Client approves final in portal Revision stage → complete → final invoice becomes visible/payable on client portal

Step-by-Step

1
Check intake in the Project Hub
Open the project → scroll to "Intake" section. Read every field. Download any files the client uploaded (logos, photos, content). If anything is missing or unclear, message the client directly before building — not after.
2
Create a new Vercel project for this client
Name it client-name-site or client-name-tool. Never share Vercel projects between clients. Push to a new GitHub repo under the same naming convention. Every git push auto-deploys a preview URL you can share.
3
Build Draft 1 — aim for 80% complete
Draft 1 should show the full structure, layout, branding, and content — not a wireframe, but not pixel-perfect either. The goal is to let the client react to something real. Focus on: correct pages, correct copy, correct colors/fonts. Don't polish until after feedback.
4
Send Draft 1 from the Project Hub
Go to Project Hub → Intake section → paste your Vercel preview URL in "Draft Preview URL" → click "Send Draft 1 →". The client gets an email immediately and sees a green CTA on their portal to review it.
5
Wait for client feedback (check Dashboard daily)
The Dashboard "Needs Attention" section lights up when the client submits their feedback form. Do not check in with clients asking "did you review it?" — the system notifies them. If it's been 5+ business days with no response, a gentle email reminder is appropriate.
6
Apply changes → Send Draft 2
Read all feedback from the intake submission (visible in the Project Hub). Apply every change they requested that is within scope. Update your Vercel project — the same URL updates automatically. Click "Send Draft 2 →" in the Project Hub.
7
Apply final changes → Send Final
Draft 2 feedback comes in. Apply changes. This is the "Final" version — it should be 100% polished and ready to launch. Click "Send Final →". Client sees "Approve Final →" on their portal.
8
Client approves → Phase 5 begins
When the client clicks "Approve" on the portal, the revision stage moves to "complete." The final invoice becomes visible and payable on their portal. Dashboard will no longer show this project under "Needs Attention" — it moves to payment tracking.
Timing Expectations to Set with Clients Tell clients upfront: "Draft 1 in 5–7 business days from intake. Draft 2 in 3–5 business days from your feedback. Final in 2–3 business days." Always use ranges, never hard dates. Build 1–2 buffer days in for your own sanity.
Rules

Revision Rules & Scope Discipline

Your contract includes 2 revisions. This section defines what counts as a revision, what's included at no cost, and when to use the "Add Scope Items" tool to charge for extras.

What's Included in the Base Price
  • Up to 2 rounds of revisions (Draft 1 → Draft 2 → Final)
  • Changes to text/copy that the client provided or corrected
  • Color and font adjustments within their stated brand
  • Layout tweaks that don't require new pages or new sections
  • Bug fixes — anything broken on your end is always free
  • Mobile responsiveness fixes
What's a Scope Add-On (Billable)
  • New pages not included in the original intake
  • A third round of revisions after Final is already approved
  • New features not in the original scope (contact forms, booking, galleries)
  • Content writing — if they didn't provide copy and want you to write it
  • Logo design or brand identity work
  • Integrations that weren't discussed (CRMs, APIs, Zapier, etc.)
  • Rushed delivery (24–48 hour turnaround)
How to Add a Scope Add-On (Mid-Project)
1
Tell the client verbally first
"That's outside the original scope — I can add it for $X. Want me to include it?" Get verbal agreement before billing.
2
Use "Add Scope Items" in Project Hub
Open the project → Scope of Work section → "Add Scope Items" button. Select from catalog or enter custom item. This creates the invoice, generates a Stripe link, appends a contract amendment, and emails the client — all in one click.
3
Don't build until invoice is paid
Add-on work doesn't begin until the add-on invoice is paid. This is a hard rule — it protects you from building unbilled scope.
⚠️ Golden Rule: No Scope, No Build If it's not in the signed contract or a paid add-on invoice, you don't build it. Every exception trains clients that your price isn't real. Be firm, be kind, and use the add-on flow — it's built exactly for this.
When Clients Ask for "Just One More Thing" The right response: "I can definitely add that — it'll be [catalog item or custom price]. Let me send you an add-on invoice and we'll get it done right after." This reframes it as a positive (you're helping them get what they want) rather than a denial.
Phase 5

Final Payment & Handoff

Once the client approves the final version, two things happen in sequence: they pay the final invoice, then you transfer ownership. Nothing gets transferred before payment is confirmed.

Step 1 — Final Invoice & Payment
1
Confirm the final invoice is in the Project Hub
The final invoice was created at onboarding (50% of total). Verify the amount is correct. If it was updated with add-ons, make sure the add-on invoices are separate — the final invoice only covers the original balance.
2
Client pays via portal
Once approved, the client's portal automatically shows "Pay your final invoice →". They click, pay via Stripe. The webhook fires and automatically marks the invoice paid + marks the client "completed" in the system.
3
Confirm payment in Stripe dashboard
Don't rely only on the webhook. Log in to Stripe and confirm the payment settled. Especially important for first-time clients.
Step 2a — Website Handoff (Website & Website + Tool projects)
1
Client submits launch details via portal
After paying, the portal shows "Complete your launch details →". The client enters their Vercel account email and (optionally) their GitHub username. You see this in the Project Hub → Launch section.
2
Add client as collaborator on Vercel
Go to the Vercel project → Settings → Members → invite their email. This gives them viewer access to their own deployment without full account control.
3
Connect their custom domain
In Vercel project → Settings → Domains → add their domain. Give them the DNS records (A record + CNAME) to add in their registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google Domains, etc.). Once propagated, their site is live at their own domain.
4
Transfer the GitHub repo (if client wants it)
GitHub → repo → Settings → Danger Zone → Transfer. Transfer to the client's GitHub account using their username from the launch form. This gives them full ownership of the codebase.
5
Mark as Launched in Project Hub
Project Hub → Launch section → "Mark as Launched ✓". Project stage moves to "launched." Client portal shows "🎉 Project Complete!" The project is archived from active tracking.
Step 2b — Tool Delivery (Tool-only projects)
1
Prepare delivery package
After final payment, prepare everything the client needs: the live tool URL (already deployed on your Vercel), access credentials or API keys they need, a short walkthrough video (Loom — 5 min max), and any documentation or user guide.
2
Send delivery email
Email the client directly with everything they need: the live URL, login instructions, and a link to your Loom walkthrough. Keep it concise — one email with all the info, not a series of back-and-forths.
3
Mark as Delivered in Project Hub
Project Hub → Launch section → "Mark as Delivered ✓". Same result — project goes to "launched" and the client portal shows completion.
⚠️ Hard Rule: No Handoff Before Full Payment Never give domain access, transfer the repo, or share the final Vercel project with a client until all invoices (including add-ons) show as paid in the system. Stripe's webhook handles the status automatically — trust the system.
What If the Client Hasn't Paid in 7+ Days? Send one professional follow-up: "Hi [name], just checking in — your final invoice is ready in your portal and your project is fully approved! Let me know if you have any questions about payment." If no response after 14 days, send a firm reminder and note that project files will not be transferred until payment is received. Always keep it professional.
Phase 6

Monthly Care Plan — $150/mo

The Care Plan is recurring revenue. It's the difference between one-time project income and a stable monthly base. Every completed client should be offered a Care Plan before the project fully closes.

What's Included — $150/month
Service Details
Hosting oversight You monitor their Vercel deployment. If anything goes down, you know before they do.
Uptime monitoring Basic uptime alerts (use UptimeRobot free tier or Vercel monitoring).
Minor content updates Up to 1 hour per month of content changes: text updates, new photos, small copy edits, adding a service, updating hours/pricing. Anything over 1 hour is billed at your hourly rate ($75/hr).
Priority support Care Plan clients get a 24-hour response guarantee vs. standard 3–5 business days for one-off requests.
Annual security review Once per year: dependency updates, check for exposed API keys, review Supabase RLS policies if applicable.
What's NOT Included (Billed Separately)
  • New pages or major redesigns — quote as a new project
  • New tools or features — use the scope add-on flow
  • Content writing or copywriting
  • Content updates exceeding 1 hour/month — billed at $75/hr overage
  • Domain registration or renewals (client's responsibility)
  • Third-party service fees (email marketing tools, CRMs, etc.)
How to Offer It (The Right Moment)
1
During onboarding (ideal)
The ScopeSelector already has a Care Plan checkbox. If the client checks it during onboarding, it's added to the contract. Monthly billing begins 30 days after launch. This is the smoothest path — they're already saying yes to everything else.
2
At project close (second-best)
When you send the "project complete" message or the client's portal shows 🎉, mention it: "By the way, I offer a Care Plan for $150/month that covers hosting oversight, minor updates, and priority support. Want me to include it going forward?" This is a natural, low-pressure moment.
3
Any time they ask for a quick update
If a client (not on Care Plan) asks for a content change, quote the hourly rate — then mention that the Care Plan would cover changes like that for $150/month. Let the value comparison sell itself.
How to Set Up Billing
1
Create a recurring product in Stripe
Stripe Dashboard → Products → Create product → "3DT Care Plan" → $150/month recurring → Generate a payment link. You'll reuse this link for every Care Plan client (or create per-client subscriptions for better tracking).
2
Send them the Stripe subscription link
Share the payment link via email or text. When they subscribe, Stripe auto-bills monthly on their card. No manual invoicing required.
3
Note it in the admin app
Edit the project or client notes in the admin app and add "Care Plan: Active since [month]". Keeps your records clean for when they ask about their subscription later.
The Numbers — Why Care Plans Matter 5 Care Plan clients = $750/month in recurring revenue. 10 = $1,500/month. 20 = $3,000/month. At $1,500 revenue per project average, that's the equivalent of one extra project per month — without doing any new sales. Prioritize building this number early.
